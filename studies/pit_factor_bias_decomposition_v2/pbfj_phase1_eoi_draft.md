**Subject:** Expression of Interest - Hybrid Pre-Registration - Information Timing and A-Share Factor Evidence

Dear Professor Faff and the Pacific-Basin Finance Journal editorial team,

We seek guidance on the suitability of a hybrid pre-registration proposal titled **"How Much Do Information Timing and Implementation Choices Shift A-Share Factor Evidence? A Pre-Specified Bias Decomposition."**

The project asks a measurement question in empirical asset pricing: how much do final-survivor sampling, report-period rather than publication-date availability, tradability screens, liquidity requirements, and implementation timing change familiar A-share factor evidence? The proposed contribution is not a new factor or a search for the best-performing specification. It is an all-results-reported decomposition of the bias introduced by the historical information set and execution clock.

We have already completed and publicly disclosed a short pilot using 2023-2026 data and covering 18 evaluation cross-sections from January 2025 through June 2026. The pilot was repository-locked but not externally preregistered. It reports all 16 pre-specified cells and shows that publication-date alignment materially attenuates ROE and a fixed-weight composite. We recognize that the entire pilot is observed prior information. It will be reported separately as an empirical precondition and will not enter the Stage-2 primary estimate or any claim of prospective confirmation.

Our proposed Stage-2 design treats the pilot only as a transparent empirical precondition. Before accessing Stage-2 outcomes, we would freeze and externally timestamp:

- one primary estimand: the paired monthly signed ROE rank-IC decrement, defined as publication-date IC minus report-period-end IC within the point-in-time universe;
- a SH/SZ A-share historical outcome panel targeting January 2010 through December 2022, with no factor outcomes inspected before a signed exposure attestation and journal-authorized freeze;
- the fixed-weight composite as a secondary timing estimand and momentum and low volatility as timing negative controls;
- a complete 2^4 factorial of ST exclusion, suspension exclusion, a liquidity floor, and a one-session lag;
- exact Shapley attribution limited to that four-component implementation block, paired HAC inference, exactly 26 inferential estimands (one primary plus a fixed 25-test secondary Benjamini-Hochberg family), two separate deterministic timing-isolation checks, and all-cell reporting;
- a prior-exposure log, with a structured deviation-log and receipt-reporting module explicitly planned but not yet implemented;
- a code, plan, file-hash, and result receipt.

The 2010-2022 target interval is a fixed design boundary, not a menu from which a favorable subperiod can be selected. If its outcome-blind coverage, publication-date, membership, rights, prior-exposure, or code gates fail, the Stage-2 study stops; the team will not shorten the interval after seeing outcomes. The current licensed quote bundle begins in 2023 and therefore cannot produce the historical return outcomes. Some 2020-2022 fundamental rows have been available for outcome-blind feasibility review, which will be disclosed; no 2010-2022 factor outcome panel has been assembled. Historical quote outcomes will be acquired or released to the analysis team only after the protocol is approved and frozen. The raw data are proprietary; we propose public schemas, extraction specifications, synthetic fixtures, code, immutable hashes, aggregate coverage, a one-command authorized rerun, and an independent licensed replication or reviewer-access arrangement.

The current executable Stage-2 core is deliberately limited to the complete IC cell matrix, paired IC estimands, HAC inference, and the four-component IC Shapley decomposition. The 72 cell means, t-statistics, and top-minus-universe spreads are complete descriptive outputs, not 72 discovery tests; confirmatory inference is restricted to the sole primary and fixed BH-25 family. Dedicated signal-missingness tables, exclusion reason codes, eligible-universe-loss and percentage-attenuation outputs, raw-ratio regressions, robustness analyses, next-open portfolios, transaction costs, nonfills, stationary bootstrap intervals, and factorial interaction tests are planned but not yet implemented. They will not be described as registered or executable evidence unless they are completed, tested, and frozen before the journal authorizes outcome access. The Shapley allocation preserves interactions inside the four-component block; it does not constitute a formal interaction test.

If the journal invites a later phase, the frozen execution-plan core would first be bound in a non-self-referential design manifest; an external provider receipt would then bind that manifest hash; and a separate execution authorization would verify the chronology before blind-data release. Only then would the final plan envelope record those backward-pointing hashes and `locked_at`, without changing the registered core. Where a provider offers no digital signature, a named human verifier and hash-bound provider record form an explicit trust boundary. This EOI remains an unsent draft and none of those steps has occurred.

Before preparing the next phase, we would appreciate guidance on five points:

1. Does the disclosed pilot fit the empirical-precondition concept in the 2026 hybrid pre-registration pathway?
2. At which phase of the hybrid pathway should the independent custodian release the sealed 2010-2022 panel, and does that require Phase-3 in-principle acceptance rather than an external timestamp alone?
3. What reviewer-access arrangement is expected when licensed market data cannot be redistributed?
4. May the hybrid pathway be considered for the current Responsible and Open Science initiative, or should it proceed through the journal's general preregistration route?
5. Should the proposed submission remain an IC-core study, or would the journal expect the planned portfolio, bootstrap, and interaction modules to be implemented before a Phase-3 Preregistered Report?

We would be pleased to provide the draft statistical analysis plan, pilot receipt, code repository, coverage-gate report, and a concise research pitch.
